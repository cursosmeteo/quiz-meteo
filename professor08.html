🏆 Final Quiz — Session 08

Mountain Waves · Turbulence · CAT  ·  Professor view
Question 1 / 10
Which condition is NOT required for the formation of mountain waves?
AWind speed ≥15 kt at mountain level
BWind direction within 30° of perpendicular to the ridge
CStable layer at or just above the ridge
DWind speed must increase with altitude above the ridge
Mountain waves require wind ≥15 kt perpendicular to the ridge and a stable layer at ridge level. Increasing wind speed above the ridge is not a strict requirement.
Question 2 / 10
A lenticular cloud (ACSL — Altocumulus Standing Lenticular) appears stationary because:
AAir continuously flows through it, marking a fixed wave crest
BThe cloud is frozen at high altitude
CThe wave has dissipated and only the cloud remains
DThe cloud forms in the rotor zone
A lenticular cloud marks a fixed wave crest — air flows through continuously, condensing upwind and evaporating downwind.
Question 3 / 10
The MOST dangerous zone for light aircraft in mountain wave conditions is:
ADirectly above the mountain peak
BBetween the cap cloud and lenticular cloud
CThe rotor zone, below the first wave crest in the lee
DThe tropopause, where wave amplitude is highest
The rotor zone contains severe chaotic turbulence with reversed surface flow. It is the most hazardous zone, especially for light aircraft.
Question 4 / 10
Mechanical turbulence intensity depends primarily on:
AAir temperature and humidity
BWind speed and surface roughness
CLapse rate and cloud base height
DPressure gradient and Coriolis force
Mechanical turbulence depends on wind speed and surface roughness. Stronger wind and rougher terrain increase turbulence intensity.
Question 5 / 10
Thermal turbulence is most severe when:
ASurface heating is strong and the atmosphere is unstable
BA temperature inversion is present at low level
CWind speed is low and relative humidity is high
DCloud cover is complete, preventing solar radiation
Strong solar heating combined with atmospheric instability generates vigorous convective currents producing severe thermal turbulence.
Question 6 / 10
Frontal turbulence is typically associated with:
AMountain ridges perpendicular to the wind
BWind shear along the frontal surface and embedded Cb
CClear air at cruise level above FL 350
DAnticyclonic flow at the tropopause
Frontal turbulence results from wind shear along the frontal boundary and embedded Cb. Cold fronts produce the most severe cases.
Question 7 / 10
CAT (Clear Air Turbulence) is defined as turbulence:
AInside cumulonimbus clouds above FL 250
BIn the boundary layer, caused by surface heating
CAbove 15 000 ft in cloud-free air, associated with wind shear
DBelow the tropopause, in frontal cloud
CAT occurs above 15 000 ft in clear air with no visual warning. It is caused by wind shear near the jet stream and cannot be detected by weather radar.
Question 8 / 10
The PRIMARY CAT zone relative to the polar jet stream is located on the:
AWarm-air side, above the jet core
BWarm-air side, below the jet core
CCold-air side, above the jet core
DCold-air side, just below the jet core
The primary CAT zone is on the cold-air (poleward) side, just below the jet core, where vertical wind shear is greatest.
Question 9 / 10
When encountering unexpected severe turbulence in cruise, the immediate priority is to:
AIncrease speed to maintain control authority
BDescend below the turbulence layer immediately
CReduce to turbulence penetration speed (VB) and inform ATC
DSwitch off autopilot and hand-fly at maximum cruise speed
Reduce to VB immediately to avoid structural damage, turn on seat-belt signs, and notify ATC. Increasing speed risks overstressing the airframe.
Question 10 / 10
Which is the best visual indicator of severe mountain wave activity?
ACumulonimbus with anvil shape
BRotor cloud below the wave crest combined with lenticular clouds above
CStratus layers covering the mountain top
DCirrus uncinus (mare’s tails) at high altitude
The combination of rotor cloud (low, turbulent) and lenticular clouds (stationary, lens-shaped) is the classic visual signature of active mountain wave activity.
