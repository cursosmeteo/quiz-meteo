Context
What is KSA Assessment?

KSA stands for Knowledge, Skills and Attitudes — the three dimensions used by EASA and ICAO to evaluate pilot competence beyond the traditional written examination. While written tests assess factual recall, KSA assessment evaluates whether a student can apply knowledge in realistic operational scenarios, make sound decisions under time pressure, and demonstrate the professional attitudes expected of a licenced pilot.

In the context of EASA ATPL training, KSA assessment is progressively replacing purely academic evaluation methods. The aim is to ensure that future airline pilots do not merely know the theory — they must be able to integrate multiple sources of information, reason under uncertainty, communicate effectively, and make defensible operational decisions.

K
Knowledge
Factual and conceptual understanding of meteorological principles, codes, and procedures.
  • ATPL theory — syllabus 050
  • METAR / TAF / SIGMET decoding
  • Chart interpretation
  • Hazard identification
S
Skills
Ability to apply knowledge to real-world scenarios and make operationally sound decisions.
  • Multi-source data integration
  • GO / DELAY / NO-GO reasoning
  • Risk assessment and mitigation
  • Structured oral briefing
A
Attitudes
Professional behaviours and CRM competencies demonstrated throughout the exercise.
  • Participation and engagement
  • Team coordination (CRM)
  • Response to new information
  • Communication clarity
EASA ATPL Subject 050
Why Meteorology for KSA?

Meteorology is uniquely suited to KSA assessment because it requires the integration of multiple, often conflicting sources of information — METARs, TAFs, SIGMETs, PIREPs, satellite imagery, radar, upper wind charts, SIGWX — each with different validity periods, geographic coverage, and levels of reliability. No single source tells the full story.

A real pre-flight meteorological briefing demands exactly the competencies that KSA assessment targets: the ability to decode and interpret technical data, synthesise it into a coherent situational picture, assess risk against operational constraints, reach a defensible decision, and communicate it clearly to crew and dispatch. These are Skills and Attitudes that cannot be evaluated by a multiple-choice examination alone.

Furthermore, meteorology is dynamic — conditions change between the time a TAF is issued and the actual departure, a SIGMET may be amended, a PIREP may contradict the forecast. The ability to integrate updated information and revise an operational decision accordingly is a core Attitude competency directly linked to flight safety.

⚠️ Operational relevance
Weather-related accidents and incidents remain a leading cause of aviation occurrences worldwide. EASA's competency-based training framework (Part-FCL, AMC1 ORA.ATO.120) specifically requires that meteorological decision-making be assessed in realistic, scenario-based contexts — not only in written examinations.
Exercise Structure
How the Session Works

METEO BRIEF is a timed, collaborative simulation exercise. Each session uses a real flight route defined by the instructor. Students work first individually, then in groups, to analyse the current meteorological situation and reach an operational decision — all within a structured timeline visible to everyone.

Phase 1
8 min
Pre-Test MCQ Individual
10 ATPL meteorology questions, 20 seconds each. Scored automatically. Results determine group formation.
Phase 2
3 min
Group Formation Automatic
Snake distribution by score. Highest scorer per group = Leader. Groups receive their shared screen QR code.
Phase 3
5 min
Mission Briefing Instructor
Route, ETD, ETA, FL and aircraft presented. Students receive access to live meteorological sources.
Phase 4
25 min
Individual Met Analysis Individual
Each student independently accesses live met sources and completes an analysis form: primary hazard, severity, operational impact, preliminary GO/DELAY/NO-GO, and sources consulted.
Phase 5
20 min
Decision Table + Group Decision Group
Groups discuss individual analyses and complete a structured Decision Table covering DEP, en-route and ARR conditions. They then submit a collective GO / DELAY / NO-GO decision with full justification.
Phase 6
10 min
Injected Challenge Group
Instructor injects a new event — updated SIGMET, route change, or time pressure. Groups revise or confirm their decision with justification.
Phase 7
5 min
Briefing Key MCQ Group
3 questions to unlock oral briefing at no cost. Wrong answers incur a point penalty per member (−10 / −20 / −30).
Phase 8
~2 min/grp
Oral Briefing Jury
Each group presents a structured 2-minute meteorological briefing. Each student is scored individually by the instructor on Accuracy, Decision Justified, and Communication Clarity.
Phase 9
10 min
Results & Debrief All
Final KSA scores displayed. Instructor leads debrief comparing group decisions and hazard identification quality.

📋 Sources consulted — mandatory for oral briefing
Students must record all meteorological sources consulted during their analysis (name + URL). These are submitted as part of the group Decision Table and must be cited during the oral briefing. This reflects the operational requirement for a traceable, auditable pre-flight weather assessment.
